Technical System Architecture & Evaluation Summary

1. Hydrologic Problem Statement: Urban storm events trigger sudden, severe deoxygenation in freshwater streams due to combined sewer overflows (CSOs), benthic sediment resuspension, and elevated runoff temperatures. Decision-makers lack tools combining predictive ML with calibrated uncertainty and actionable safe boundaries.
2. Intended Operational Users: Municipal watershed managers, environmental protection agencies, stormwater utility operators, and aquatic conservation teams.
3. Pipeline Architecture: End-to-end Python scientific pipeline (Stages 1–7): USGS/ERA5 Ingestion → LightGBM Model B → 2D Storm Tolerance Envelope → Empirical Action Window → Streeter-Phelps Intervention Solver → Privacy-Preserving Field Verification.
4. Machine-Learning Methodology: LightGBM gradient boosting regressor forecasting 72-hour minimum DO from 156 backward-looking features. Quantile regressors (q10, q50, q90) guarantee non-crossing intervals, calibrated via inductive split conformal inference (90% guaranteed coverage).
5. Storm Tolerance Envelope (Centerpiece): Empirical safe boundary R_safe = f(T_water) establishing maximum permissible rainfall before threshold breach probability exceeds operational risk limit alpha. Governed by a Delaunay convex hull domain refusal guardrail.
6. Intervention Rehearsal & Lag Timing: Empirical lag distributions establish operational decision deadlines (P10 = 6.5h, P50 = 10.0h). Continuous Streeter-Phelps mass balance rehearses 4 response archetypes with logistical feasibility gating.
7. Field Verification & One Health: Dual-track validation ledger segregates citizen observations from certified sensors. Coordinate coarsening (~1.1 km) protects privacy. Human-contact risk is marked NOT ASSESSED without laboratory microbial assays.

Full Scientific Limitations Disclosure

1. Single Station Calibration: Calibrated strictly on USGS 01474500 (Schuylkill River at Philadelphia). Not validated for headwater tributaries.
2. No Observed DO Breaches Below 5.0 mg/L: In our 58-event catalog (2023–2026), the lowest observed storm minimum was 5.65 mg/L (Event 54). Extreme breach risk curves represent statistical extrapolations rather than empirical collapses.
3. Preliminary Risk Boundary: The safe boundary curve is an empirical decision contour, not a rigid physical failure threshold.
4. Action Window Uncertainty: The lag-to-nadir bounds are empirical percentiles, not a deterministic countdown timer.
5. Unvalidated Simulation: Intervention benefits (+0.77 mg/L) are calculated via continuous Streeter-Phelps mass-balance equations; no in-situ engineering deployment occurred.
6. Daytime Observation Bias: Citizen submissions are clustered between 08:00–20:00 UTC, missing pre-dawn oxygen sags.
7. Missing Microbial Assays: Dissolved oxygen cannot verify pathogen safety. Human contact risk is NOT ASSESSED without laboratory bacterial culture data.
